3Dminus1 exercise 1

Google's 3D map is made of 3D shapes wrapped in flat images called textures. We download the 3D map of an area, throw the shapes away and keep only the textures, as if every building had been smashed flat. Do the steps in order: each button unlocks the next one.

After Clement Valla, 3D-Maps-Minus-3D: Google Earth's 3D map shown with all the 3D removed, leaving only the flat texture images that normally wrap its buildings, roads and hills.

0 Your key

Paste the Google key your teacher gave you. This browser remembers it.

1 Choose an area and find its 3D tiles

Type a place name, or go to bboxfinder.com, draw a rectangle and copy the numbers from the Box field. Keep it small (a few hundred metres).

The numbers already filled in are the Schlachthof block in Zürich.

2 Download the tiles, keep only the textures

Every tile is a small 3D model plus some images. We keep the images and ignore the 3D.

3 Smash the city flat

Each texture is pasted on the map where its tile stands.

4 Save everything

You get one .zip with map.jpg and a squares folder: every texture, named after the coordinates of its block (latitude_longitude.jpg). Paste the two numbers into Google Maps, with a comma between them, to see the spot.
